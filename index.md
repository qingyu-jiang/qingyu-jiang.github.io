# I study how family relationships and everyday support shape mental health and child development.
{: .intro-heading }

My research spans the perinatal period and infancy through childhood, tracing how early experiences relate to adolescent well-being. I examine coparenting, caregiver mental health, and resources that support children and families facing stress and socioeconomic disadvantage.

I use longitudinal analyses, qualitative inquiry, and mixed methods to study these questions. An emerging area of my work examines how parents use generative AI and how it fits into their existing sources of information and support.

Before joining the University of Idaho, I completed a postdoctoral fellowship at Brigham and Women’s Hospital/Harvard Medical School.
